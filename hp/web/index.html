How HarvestProof works

Plain answers to what each part does, and what it does not do.

The leaf check (not a neural network)

The photo is split into leaf, spots, yellow and white areas. We measure how many spots there are and whether they are long and thin or round. A trained model turns those measurements into probabilities. Your answers to simple questions are added as extra evidence, and we always ask the question that removes the most doubt. If the evidence is not clear we say uncertain instead of guessing.

Photo analysis exists for tea and chilli, trained on public photos. Rice and grapes use your answers only, because we do not have photos for them yet.

The pack check

Each pack code ends in a signature that only the issuer can create, so forged or mistyped codes fail. We also check the batch is registered and in date, and flag a code that has been scanned in many districts within a short time, which suggests copies.

The spray options

Products and waiting periods come from India's official list of registered pesticide uses. We only show products whose label names your problem on your crop, drop those that would make you harvest too early, and avoid repeating the same mode of action (this slows resistance).

The passport

Safe harvest day = last spray date + the longest label waiting period. It also warns when the EU limit for a product is very strict, because meeting India's waiting period does not guarantee passing an EU residue test.

Words used here

Waiting period: days that must pass between the last spray and harvest. Mode of action: how a chemical kills the disease. Using the same one repeatedly makes diseases resistant. Residue limit (MRL): the most chemical allowed on food that is sold.

What it is not

It is advice, not a laboratory certificate, and not a replacement for an agriculture officer. Photo accuracy is measured on public dataset photos and will be lower on your own field photos. Label data was transcribed from official lists and must be checked against the product label.
